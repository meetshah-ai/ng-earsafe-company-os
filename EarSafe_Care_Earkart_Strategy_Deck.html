Who we sell to

We don't sell to the patient.
We sell to their child.

In India the hearing aid is researched, chosen and paid for by the adult child (30–45) — for a parent (55–75). They are online, low on stigma, and already inside EarSafe's audience.

  • Primary: adult child buying for a parent
  • Secondary: early-onset self-buyer, 45–60 (invisible + rechargeable hooks)
  • Pipeline: our younger, noise-aware audience — screening today, customers tomorrow

The Apple clock — why we aim here

AirPods Pro's hearing-aid feature is not yet approved in India — but when it lands it takes the mild-loss, young, tech self-buyer. So we plant our flag where Apple structurally won't go: moderate loss, the older parent, and the value of a human fitting and warranty.

The commercial model

Clean split. Each does what they do best.

Supply

Earkart supplies the certified range at the agreed landed cost. EarSafe holds MRP — a value-leader, never a discounter.

Channel

Own D2C only for the pilot — EarSafe Care site + WhatsApp. No marketplaces; we protect the brand and the certified story.

Pricing posture

Win on spec-per-rupee + the ₹25K benefit stack. Entry SKUs (Fame ₹7,900) as EMI-friendly door-openers.

The principle that protects both sides

EarSafe earns the device margin for bringing qualified demand; Earkart contractually owns fitting, warranty and returns liability. Margin never moves without the clinical responsibility moving with it. That is what makes selling a medical device online safe — for the customer and for both brands.
