## 01. BRAND FOUNDATION

### The Core Belief
> The way we have been listening is hurting us. And we did not notice because everyone was doing it.

This is not a tagline. This is the reason the brand exists. Every piece of content, every product decision, every word the brand says must be traceable back to this belief.

### The Founding Story
In 2020, while the world locked itself inside, we locked ourselves into our earphones.

Back-to-back calls. Music to survive the silence. Earphones in from morning to midnight because the outside world had disappeared and the inside world needed to be filled with something.

Our founder lived this. Hours on calls with earphones that were never designed for hours of calls. An ear canal that began to protest — quietly at first, then loudly enough that it was impossible to ignore. Pain that became normal because everyone around him had the same pain and nobody was doing anything about it.

That normalisation is what started everything. Not the pain itself. The acceptance of the pain.

He went looking for an answer. What he found was open ear technology — headphones that sit outside the ear canal entirely, letting you hear your music and your world at the same time, without the pressure, the heat, the bacteria, the isolation of a sealed ear.

He found that this technology existed. That it worked. That almost nobody in India knew about it.

In August 2020, with twelve units and one belief, NG EarSafe launched.

Four years and 14,000+ customers later, the brand makes the full range of open ear audio for India — from the most affordable wired open ear earphone in the market to the AI-enabled SafeBuds ecosystem.

One belief drives everything: **the world is worth listening to. All of it.**

### The Brand Mission
To end the quiet epidemic of earphone-induced hearing loss in India — not through fear or guilt, but by making open ear listening so desirable, so accessible, and so clearly better that the switch feels inevitable.

### The Brand Promise
**You never have to choose.**
Not music or the world. Not awareness or immersion. Not comfort or performance. With EarSafe, the answer is always: both.

### The One-Line Position
> NG EarSafe is India's open ear brand — for people who want to be in their life, not just in their head.

## 02. THE CAUSE

### Why This Matters Beyond the Product
The WHO estimates 1.1 billion young people globally are at risk of hearing loss from recreational audio exposure. India, with its explosive growth in personal audio and its culture of high-volume, long-duration listening, is among the most at-risk markets on earth.

Earphone-induced hearing loss is:
- Permanent. Damaged hair cells in the cochlea do not regenerate.
- Invisible. There is no pain signal until significant damage has already occurred.
- Accelerating. TWS adoption in India grew 3x between 2020 and 2024.
- Preventable. Open ear listening eliminates the primary risk factor entirely.

### How the Cause Appears in Communication
The cause is never used as a guilt mechanism. The brand does not scare people into switching. It makes them curious, then desirous, then converts them.

The cause runs underneath every piece of content as the reason — it surfaces directly in approximately 20% of posts, and indirectly in everything else through the philosophy of presence, openness, and listening to the world.

The communication ratio is: **60% life and philosophy / 30% product desire / 10% direct conversion.**

## 03. THE PRODUCT RANGE

Understanding the full product family is essential for accurate content creation.

### Product Architecture

**Everyday Tier — Open ear as default**
- **EarSafe Lite** — Entry-level open ear. Bone conduction or OWS depending on variant. First step out of the sealed ear. Audience: first-time switchers, value-conscious.
- **OpenWire** — Wired open ear. No battery anxiety. Pure simplicity. Audience: those who want reliability over wireless.

**Professional Tier — For people whose voice is their work**
- **EarSafe Comm / Comm 2.0** — Purpose-built for long call days. All-day wear comfort. Audience: WFH professionals, managers, consultants, anyone on 4+ hours of calls daily.

**Performance Tier — For people who move hard**
- **EarSafe Pro** — Bone conduction. IP65 waterproof. Built for running, cycling, swimming. Audience: serious fitness users, outdoor athletes.
- **EarSafe Pro Mini** — Same performance philosophy, smaller form factor.

**Next Tier — The TWS replacement**
- **OpenBuds** — OWS (Open Wearable Stereo) form factor. TWS form, open ear philosophy. Direct challenger to AirPods and Boat Airdopes. Audience: current TWS users who have not yet discovered open ear.
- **SafeBuds** — Premium flagship. AI-enabled, live language translation, 30-hour battery, full open ear ecosystem. Audience: premium buyers, tech-forward, SafeBuds is the brand's most sophisticated expression.

### The OpenBuds / SafeBuds Positioning Rule
These products are never described as "open ear earbuds" or a variant of a TWS product. They are positioned as the evolution of the TWS category — everything TWS got right, without the one thing it got wrong.

The line: **"Everything TWS promised. Nothing it took away."**

## 04. THE AUDIENCE

Four attitudinal tribes. Not demographic segments. These are defined by worldview, not age or income bracket.

### Tribe A — The Aware Professional
**Who:** 30–50 years old. WFH or hybrid work. Back-to-back calls. Has experienced ear pain or discomfort but accepted it as normal. Values reliability and clarity. Possibly wears glasses (glasses + IEM = compounded discomfort). Buys based on comfort and call quality. Primary urban metros — Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad.

**Their unsaid thought:** "My ears hurt after 2pm every day but I don't know what to do about it."

**Product:** EarSafe Comm is the primary. OpenBuds secondary.

**What content reaches them:** Scenes from their actual day. A home office at 4pm. Six hours of calls acknowledged with warmth, not pity. The moment of relief. They respond to being seen, not being sold to.

### Tribe B — The Mindful Mover
**Who:** 25–45 years old. Runs, cycles, swims, or walks regularly. Wants music on every session. Has had at least one moment of near-danger on a run with earphones in — a vehicle they didn't hear, a turn they misjudged. Values safety but frames it as performance, not health anxiety.

**Their unsaid thought:** "I pull one earbud out every time I cross a road. That can't be right."

**Product:** EarSafe Pro primary. EarSafe Lite secondary.

**What content reaches them:** Early morning runs. The city at 6am. Motion and music and world all in frame at once. They respond to aspiration and identity, not safety messaging.

### Tribe C — The Converted Skeptic
**Who:** 22–40 years old. Was a committed TWS user — AirPods, Boat, Sony, OnePlus. Switched to open ear after fatigue, infection, discomfort, or a recommendation. Now evangelical. Frames their old earphones as a past mistake they have moved beyond.

**Their unsaid thought:** "I can't believe I used in-ear headphones for five years. I'll never go back."

**Product:** OpenBuds and SafeBuds primary. Everything else secondary — they want the most open-ear-forward product in the range.

**What content reaches them:** Content that validates their switch. The AirPods crowd framed with gentle wit. The TWS compromise named accurately. They share this content because it confirms their identity as someone who made a better choice.

### Tribe D — The Presence Seeker
**Who:** 20–35 years old. Culturally curious. Values being fully present — in conversations, at concerts, with family. Does not frame their audio choices as a health decision — frames them as a life philosophy. Tech-aware but not tech-obsessed. Follows brands that have a point of view.

**Their unsaid thought:** "I want great sound that doesn't make me feel cut off from everything around me."

**Product:** SafeBuds as the aspirational flagship. All products serve this audience but SafeBuds is their expression.

**What content reaches them:** Brand world content. Philosophy. The feeling of being fully in a moment. They follow the brand before they buy the product.

## 05. BRAND CHARACTER

### Who NG EarSafe Is as a Person
This character is the internal filter for every content and communication decision. Before publishing anything, ask: would this person say this?

They grew up in a mid-sized Indian city. Moved to Bangalore or Mumbai for work. Curious about the world in a genuine, unhurried way. Listens to a mix of Indian classical, Prateek Kuhad, and Tame Impala. Reads — not to signal it, but because they actually enjoy it. Runs, but doesn't post run data. Cooks on weekends. Has opinions but doesn't need to perform them.

When they believe in something, they are quietly evangelical — they mention it once, clearly, and trust the idea is good enough to land. They do not shout. Do not discount. Do not explain technology to people who did not ask. Do not make anyone feel guilty for not knowing something.

They are warm without being soft. Confident without being arrogant. Curious without being scattered. Indian without being a caricature of it.

### Brand Voice — Four Dimensions

**1. Warm, not clinical**
Write like someone who genuinely cares about the reader. Never like a health warning. Never like a spec sheet.

**2. Grounded, not preachy**
The brand has a mission around hearing loss but never lectures. It earns the right to the conversation through warmth and accuracy, not through guilt.

**3. Curious, not clever**
The brand notices things. Asks real questions. Finds the overlooked angle. It is not trying to be witty — it is trying to be genuinely interesting. Wit is the occasional outcome of genuine curiosity, not the goal.

**4. Confident without arrogance**
The brand knows open ear is better. It does not need to shout this. It states what it believes and lets the idea work. It never positions against competitors by name with aggression.

## 06. VERBAL IDENTITY

### The Vocabulary

**Words the brand owns and uses:**
open · present · aware · expansive · both · stay · hear · world · alive · flow · real · everyday · in it · together · clear · light · breath · honest

**Words the brand never uses:**
safe (overused, clinical) · technology (generic) · innovation (hollow) · premium (table stakes) · advanced (meaningless) · revolutionary (every brand says this) · cutting-edge · next-generation · game-changing

### The Five Caption Personality Modes

Every post caption falls into one of these five modes. Never mix modes within a single caption.

**Mode 1 — The Observer**
The brand notices something true about everyday life. No product. No agenda. A quiet, accurate observation that makes someone feel understood.

*Used for:* Philosophy posts, brand world content, Sunday posts.

*Example:*
"The moment you pull out one earbud to hear someone better — that moment knows something about how we were built to listen."

---

**Mode 2 — The Nudge**
Gently provocative. Makes the reader reconsider something they have accepted as normal. No aggression. Just clarity and a well-aimed question.

*Used for:* Monday Reels opener, Carousel first slide, reactive posts.

*Example:*
"When did we decide that great sound required blocking the world out? That was not a rule. That was just what they sold us."

---

**Mode 3 — The Scene**
A specific, sensory moment painted in words. A person in a real place, doing a real thing. The product is present but not the subject — the feeling is the subject.

*Used for:* Lifestyle Reels, Friday posts, all AI-generated visual content.

*Example:*
"6am Bangalore. Run starting. Playlist on. Auto honking somewhere behind her. She heard all of it. That was the point."

---

**Mode 4 — The Wit**
Dry, quick, self-aware humour. One idea, one line, done. Never forced. Never try-hard. Zomato-speed.

*Used for:* Meme-format Reels, culture-reactive posts, Saturday grid posts.

*Example:*
"Your earbuds have been inside your ear canal for 6 hours. Your ear canal did not consent to this."

---

**Mode 5 — The Declaration**
Short, confident, brand-defining. No hedging. No explanation. The brand states something true and stops.

*Used for:* Brand milestone posts, product reveal slides, grid anchor posts.

*Example:*
"We have been building open ear since 2020. The world is catching up."

### Caption Anatomy — Fixed Structure for Every Post

```
LINE 1: The hook. One sentence. Must work as the "more" cut-off point.
LINE 2: The expansion. One sentence max. Optional — only if it adds, never if it explains.

[mandatory blank line here]

LINE 3: The turn. Brand voice. The line that changes the register slightly.

#ngearsafe  #openear  [2–3 contextual hashtags]
```

**Character budget:** Hook under 100 characters. Total caption under 300 characters before hashtags. If it is longer, cut it.

### Hashtag Standard

Always-on (every post, no exceptions):
`#ngearsafe` `#openear`

Rotating topical (choose 1–2 per post based on content):
`#stayinit` `#openearlife` `#safelistening` `#heareverything` `#openearheadphones`

Contextual (add based on specific post theme):
`#wfh` `#runningIndia` `#morningroutine` `#indiarunning` `#workfromhome` `#techIndia` `#mumbairunners` `#bangalorelife`

**Rules:**
- Maximum 5 hashtags per post. Never more.
- Always placed at the end of the caption, after a blank line.
- Never embedded inside the caption body.
- Never use broad hashtags: `#headphones` `#music` `#wireless` `#audio` — these are noise, not reach.
- Always lowercase.

### Language Register

English is the default for all content.

Hinglish is permitted only in Mode 4 (The Wit) and reactive culture posts, and only when it sounds like something a person would actually say in conversation. The test: if it sounds like a translation, do not use it.

Hindi-only posts: not recommended for the primary feed. Stories only, and only when the content is genuinely better in Hindi.

## 07. VISUAL IDENTITY

### The Central Visual Idea
**Open.**

Not open as a product feature — open as a feeling. Everything in the visual world has air in it. Space. Light coming through. The world visible beyond the frame. Nothing sealed, nothing closed, nothing that traps the eye.

### Color Palette — Locked

Derived directly from the brand logo. These five colors are the entire palette. Nothing outside this system appears on any brand asset.

| Name | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Navy | `#102442` | Foundation. All body text, dark backgrounds, authority moments. |
| Ocean | `#39869b` | Mid-tone. Secondary text, borders, dividers, buttons, badges. |
| Cyan | `#54c1c8` | Hero accent. The one element the eye goes to first in any composition. |
| Frost | `#E8F6F7` | Breathing color. Grid backgrounds, card surfaces, tints. |
| Chalk | `#F4F4F2` | Near-white. Body copy backgrounds, long-read contexts. |

**Color rules:**
- Cyan is never used as a full background at full saturation. It lives as an accent.
- Navy and Cyan never appear in equal weight on the same composition — one must dominate.
- No additional colors are introduced into any brand asset under any circumstances.
- The gradient from the logo (`#245e7d` → `#39869b` → `#54c1c8`) can be used on logomark reproductions only. Not on type, not on background fields, not on UI elements.

### Typography

**Primary typeface:** DM Sans or Plus Jakarta Sans
Used for: all body copy, captions, product labels, UI text, functional information.
Weights: Regular (400) for body, Medium (500) for headlines. Never Bold (700) — it creates visual noise.
Character: humanist, slightly open apertures, warm without being mechanical.

**Secondary typeface:** Lora or Playfair Display
Used for: philosophical statements, campaign headlines, brand manifesto moments, Reel end-frames, typographic anchor posts.
Weights: Regular (400) only.
Rule: serif type appears in isolation — never mixed with the primary typeface in the same line or text block.

**Type sizing on social assets:**
- Headline on image: 28–36px equivalent at final export size
- Body or caption overlay: 16–20px equivalent
- Minimum legible size at phone screen: 14px equivalent
- Text on Reels: maximum 2 text elements visible at any moment

### Logo Usage Rules

The logo has two elements: the logomark (the open arc forms) and the wordmark.

**On light backgrounds (Chalk, Frost, white):** Full color logo — navy arcs and teal arcs as designed.
**On dark backgrounds (Navy):** Navy arcs become white. Teal arcs remain Cyan `#54c1c8`.
**On mid-tone backgrounds:** Do not place the logo. Use wordmark only in white or navy.
**Clear space:** Maintain empty space around the logo equal to the height of the teal arc on all four sides. The logo is never crowded.
**The logo is never placed on a patterned, photographic, or gradient background.**

## 08. PHOTOGRAPHY & AI VISUAL DIRECTION

### The Three Non-Negotiable Visual Rules

**Rule 1 — The ear is always visible.**
Not the product floating in space. The product on a real ear, on a real person, in a real moment. The ear is the brand's most important real estate. Product-only shots are permitted only on pure product-launch posts.

**Rule 2 — The world is always in frame.**
Behind every person, the world continues. A street. A kitchen. A running trail. Office windows with city beyond. The world is never removed from the frame to spotlight the product — because the brand's entire promise is that the product keeps you in the world. Removing the world from the visual is a contradiction of the brand idea.

**Rule 3 — Real moments, not posed ones.**
Faces are relaxed and present, not performing. Bodies are mid-activity, not styled for the camera. The scene looks like something that was observed, not arranged.

### Light Quality
Cool and clear. Early morning light — 6am to 8am quality. The blue-hour clarity before the sun turns warm. Color temperature 5500K–6500K. This matches the brand palette's cool teal register. Do not use golden hour warmth — that is a different brand's palette.

Exception: late evening, post-sunset light for focus and concentration contexts (professional use case). This is a cooler blue-grey that still sits within the brand's color temperature range.

### The People in EarSafe Visuals
Urban Indian. 25–45 years old. Could be from any Indian city. Looks like they have a real life they are actually living. Not aspirational in an unreachable way — aspirational in a recognisable way. Their world looks like a version of the viewer's world, slightly more intentional.

### AI Image Generation Prompts — Standard Brief for Higgsfield

Use this brief as the consistent base for all AI-generated content. Adjust the scenario but maintain the visual DNA.

```
PROMPT BASE:
[Scenario description]. Early morning, cool clear daylight, 
urban Indian setting [specify city feel — Bangalore tech 
corridor / Mumbai seafront / Delhi park / Chennai street]. 
Person mid-activity, not posed. NG EarSafe [product name] 
visible on ear. Background shows the world continuing beyond 
the subject — [specify: street / office interior / running 
path / home kitchen]. Color palette: cool teal tones, 
chalk white surfaces, deep navy shadows. Photorealistic, 
documentary style, not commercial. 
```

**Generation count rule:** Always generate **1 image** per Higgsfield job. Do not generate 2 or more variants unless explicitly instructed in that session.

**What to avoid in all AI-generation:**
- Golden hour warmth or orange tones
- Sci-fi or abstract environments
- Product floating without a person
- Overly stylised or fashion-editorial expressions
- Props or contexts that feel foreign or non-Indian
- Full-face close-ups as the primary shot (ear and side-of-head shots are preferred for product context)

### Grid Color Rhythm
Every three posts, one typographic post — text on Chalk or Frost, no image. This creates visual breathing room.

The dominant color temperature alternates: cool-toned image → neutral/typographic → cool-toned image. Never two high-saturation posts consecutively.

Never two consecutive posts without a person. Never two consecutive product-forward posts.

## 09. INSTAGRAM POSTING MECHANICS

### Weekly Posting Skeleton — Fixed, Non-Negotiable for 30 Days

| Day | Format | Timing (IST) | Primary Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Reel | 7pm – 9pm | New audience reach. Brand world, culture, philosophy. No product sell pressure. |
| Tuesday | Static image | 8am – 10am | Insight or brand opinion. The brand has a point of view. |
| Wednesday | Carousel | 11am – 1pm | Storytelling with product as the natural conclusion. Education through narrative. |
| Thursday | Stories only | 12pm – 1pm | Community. Polls, Q&A, behind-the-scenes, reshare best comment of the week. |
| Friday | Reel | 7pm – 9pm | Lifestyle and desire. Aspirational without pressure. |
| Saturday | Static image | 11am – 1pm | Visual beauty. Something worth saving. Save rate is the KPI. |
| Sunday | Stories only | Flexible | Informal. Human. No production requirement. |

**Consistency rule:** These days and times are fixed for the first 30 days without exception. Skipping a post slot is not permitted. The algorithm requires consistent signals before it grants momentum. One missed slot costs more than it saves.

### Stories — Daily Standard
Minimum 1 story per day, maximum 5. Posted between 12pm–1pm IST.

Story rotation:
- Monday: Behind the making of Monday's Reel
- Tuesday: Poll or question connected to Tuesday's post topic
- Wednesday: A real brand moment — founder, team, or product in use
- Thursday: Reshare of the best comment from the week so far, with the brand's response
- Friday: A save-worthy line or thought from the week
- Weekend: Unpolished, informal, human — no production requirement

Every Friday story ends with a question that invites a DM reply. These DMs are simultaneously community building, audience research, and warm lead identification.

### Reel Architecture — The 3-Second Hook Rule

**0–3 seconds:** Visual hook. Must work with sound off. Something unexpected, beautiful, relatable, or quietly provocative appears in the first frame. No slow intros. No logo reveals. No brand name. The emotion or question comes first.

**3–15 seconds:** The hold. One idea developed simply. The product enters naturally, in context, in motion — never as a hard cut to a product shot.

**15 seconds to end:** The land. One closing line, text on screen. Brand voice. The brand says something true and stops. CTA (if any) lives in the caption only, never in the video.

**Audio:** Always use trending audio where available — Meta's distribution algorithm favors it. The trend must feel like a natural fit. When using original audio or ambient sound: the visual must be strong enough to carry the Reel alone.

**Text overlays in Reels:** Maximum 2 text elements visible simultaneously. Text appears at reading pace, not at rapid scroll pace. Font matches primary typeface. Never a colored text box placed over a scene — text is always Chalk on darker scenes, Navy on lighter scenes.

### The Algorithm Signal Priority

Optimise for these signals in order of importance:

1. **Saves** (strongest intent signal — weighted 3x by Meta)
2. **Comments in first 60 minutes** (primary distribution trigger)
3. **Shares to stories** (new audience acquisition signal)
4. **Watch time / swipe-through rate** (quality signal for Reels and Carousels)
5. **Profile visits from post** (intent to follow)
6. **Likes** (weakest signal — do not chase or optimise for)

### Community Management Protocol

**The 60-minute rule:** A human or agent must reply to every comment within 60 minutes of posting. This is a posting workflow step, not an afterthought. Early replies double the comment count and trigger Meta's distribution algorithm.

**Reply voice:** Warm, specific, never generic. Respond to what the person actually said. Ask one follow-up question in every reply where it is natural — questions generate additional comments.

**What to never do in replies:**
- Reply with only an emoji
- Reply "DM us for more info" as the first response
- Reply "Thanks for sharing!" or any generic acknowledgement
- Leave any comment unanswered in the first 60 minutes

### The Pseudo-Sell Architecture

The brand never runs a hard sales post. Product desire is built in stages across the content calendar.

**Stage 1 — No product:** Brand world, philosophy, emotion. The product does not appear. The feeling the product serves does.

**Stage 2 — Product present, not named:** The image shows the product on a person in a scene. The caption does not mention it. The viewer notices it.

**Stage 3 — Product present, subtly named:** One line in the caption acknowledges the product in context. Not a feature list. A moment.

**Stage 4 — Product as natural conclusion:** The viewer has been warmed through Stages 1–3. The product is now presented as the answer to something they have already felt. The sell does not feel like a sell because the desire was built before the ask.

Each 14-day content sprint cycles through all four stages.

## 10. THE STOP LIST

These post types are permanently prohibited. They dilute brand equity and perform below acceptable reach thresholds based on historical account data.

**Festival and holiday posts**
Generic Diwali, Dussehra, Republic Day, Janmashtami, or similar posts are prohibited unless the brand has a genuinely original angle that connects to the brand philosophy in one sentence. If the connection cannot be made in one sentence, the post does not get made.

**Feature-list content**
Bullet point specifications. Tick-mark capability lists. "Here is what makes us special" carousels. These perform at 400–600 reach average on a 14k account. Every specification can be expressed as a scene or a story. Do the harder work.

**Pure sale announcements**
"SALE LIVE! 15% OFF!" as a standalone post is prohibited. Promotions are communicated inside a story or a moment — the discount is the ending, never the headline.

**Gratitude and milestone posts**
"Thank you for 14k followers!" or "So grateful for the love!" posts are prohibited. A confident brand does not thank its audience for existing. Milestones can be acknowledged as forward-looking declarations — never as backward-looking gratitude performances.

**Generic product floating shots**
Product images on white backgrounds with no person, no scene, no world around them are prohibited except in the first 48 hours of a product launch. After launch, the product always exists in a life.

**Reposted UGC without a brand voice layer**
User content is valuable. Sharing it without a brand caption that adds perspective is not. UGC is evidence. The brand caption is the story that makes it mean something.

## 11. CONTENT EXPERIMENT FRAMEWORK

Every 14-day content sprint runs three experiments simultaneously. Results are read at Day 7 and Day 14.

### Experiment 1 — Audience x Content Match
Each post is tagged to one of four audience tribes (A, B, C, D) or marked as broad (E). Track which tribe-targeted content drives the most profile visits and follows. The winning tribe gets more content slots in the next sprint.

### Experiment 2 — Format Performance
Track reach, saves, and shares separately for Reels, Carousels, and Static images. The format with the best saves-to-reach ratio gets an additional slot in the following sprint.

### Experiment 3 — Sell Stage Conversion
Track profile visits and bio link clicks from posts at each of the four sell stages. Identify which stage converts casual viewers into website visitors. Adjust the stage distribution in the next sprint accordingly.

### What to Measure — The Funnel

| Metric | Where to Read It | What It Tells You |
|---|---|---|
| Non-follower reach % | Instagram Insights > Reach | Content escaping the existing audience |
| Save rate | Saves ÷ Reach | Content people want to return to |
| New followers per post | Follower count delta after each post | Which content type converts strangers |
| Profile visits from post | Instagram Insights > Profile visits | Intent to follow or buy |
| Bio link clicks | Link tool analytics | Which content drives purchase consideration |
| Comment volume (first 60 min) | Manual track at post + 60 min | Algorithm distribution signal |

## 13. THE ONE QUESTION BEFORE EVERY POST

Before any piece of content is approved or published, ask:

> "Would someone screenshot this and send it to a friend?"

If the honest answer is no — rethink the post. Not every post needs to go viral. But every post must deserve to exist on the grid. A post that does not earn its place dilutes everything around it.

## 14. QUICK REFERENCE — DECISION FILTERS

Use these as rapid checks during content creation.

**Is the post on brand?**
Does it feel like it was made by the character described in Section 05? Warm, curious, grounded, confident without arrogance?

**Is the visual correct?**
Cool-clear light quality. Person in frame. World visible in background. Color stays within the five-color palette. Product on an ear, not floating.

**Is the caption right?**
Under 300 characters. One of the five modes. Blank line before the turn. Hashtags at the end, maximum 5.

**Is it on the stop list?**
Check Section 10. If the post type appears there, it does not get made regardless of pressure or timing.

**Is the algorithm signal right?**
Does this post invite saving, sharing, or commenting? If the only response it invites is a like, reconsider.

**Is the sell stage correct for this point in the sprint?**
Check where the post sits in the 14-day cycle. Is it Stage 1, 2, 3, or 4? The sell pressure should match the stage.
