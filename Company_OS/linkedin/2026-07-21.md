## Option D — P4 Product / category — the "why wired" decision, told to a founder peer
**Hook** (160 chars)

```
A founder asked me this at dinner last week: why does NG EarSafe still make a wired open-ear pair when the whole category moved to true wireless?

The honest answer isn't nostalgia. It's a decision from 2020 I've never had a real reason to reverse.

Open-ear means the driver sits outside your ear canal. You hear your call and you hear the room — the delivery bike, the office door, someone asking you a question.

Wired removes the one thing that breaks that promise: a battery dying mid-commute or mid-call.

Six years in, that decision keeps paying off in ways I didn't plan for.

No Bluetooth means no Bluetooth lag — mic and audio stay in sync, which matters the moment a call gets serious.

It also meant we spent the engineering budget on the mic, not a battery and radio chip — call quality punches above its price. No premium tax for sounding clear.

That's what a value-conscious Indian buyer wants: don't make me choose between "works properly" and "affordable."

This week, ES Lite — built on the same thesis — hit a 7.12% conversion rate. Best number we've logged on the account so far.

We're still the only D2C brand in India built around this bet, six years and roughly 1 lakh customers in.

It would've been easier to chase the TWS wave. Every competitor did.

We didn't — seal a driver into a wireless earbud for battery and space, and you quietly give up what made open-ear worth building: mic clarity, zero lag, all of it.

What's the "outdated" decision in your product that's quietly still your biggest advantage?

## Option E — P3 Founder building-in-public — the recurring analytics bug, told with the trust withheld
**Hook** (94 chars)
> For the third time in three weeks, our own analytics quietly lied to us about our best number.

```
We track one number obsessively right now: TRUE ROAS — real GA4 revenue against real Meta spend, not the platform's own generous math.

Three times in the last three weeks, a single day's tracking has broken. Revenue landed in our reports as literally "(not set) / (not set)" — no source, no medium, unattributed. On the worst of those days, 60% of that entire day's revenue vanished into that bucket.

Each time, it fell on the last day of whatever week we were reading. Each time, it made our real ROAS look worse than it was.

The first time, I treated it as a one-off bug. The second time, I started asking questions. The third time, this week, I stopped assuming it was random.

This week's real number, even with the bad day included, cleared our own floor comfortably — 6.65x. But I don't fully trust it yet, because I don't know why the same failure keeps landing on the same kind of day.

Running a paid-media budget on a number you can't fully trust is its own risk — separate from whether the ads themselves are working.

I'd rather say that plainly than post a clean-looking ROAS chart and let you assume our tracking is solved. It isn't, yet.

This is now above the Meta account itself — an analytics-plumbing problem — and it's next on my list to actually close, not flag again.
