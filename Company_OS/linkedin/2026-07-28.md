## Option A — P1 Industry-news POV — antithesis: the industry's falling ROAS bar vs. our own floor
**Hook** (140 chars)
> Every 2026 benchmark report says 3x ROAS is strong for an Indian D2C brand on Meta. We treat 6x as the line where we start paying attention.

```
This week I read three separate 2026 industry reports on the same afternoon.

One put the average ROAS for the top 25 Indian D2C brands on Meta at roughly 3.2x.

Another called 3x "genuinely profitable" at a 55% margin, with the real break-even floor closer to 1.8x.

A third said the same campaign that ran at 3x ROAS in 2023 now limps in near 1.4x, and called that the new normal to plan around.

None of those numbers would clear our own bar.

Our floor right now is a TRUE ROAS of 6.0x, calculated against real GA4 revenue, not what the ad platform claims we made. Last week we cleared it at 6.65x. The trailing month sits at 6.84x.

We didn't get there by finding some secret audience nobody else has. We got there by refusing to call an average good enough — every campaign either clears the bar or we shut it down, no matter how healthy the account-wide number looks that week.

The industry keeps quietly resetting what "good" means as costs rise. That's a reasonable response to a harder market.

Ours has been to just not accept a lower bar because everyone else did.

If your floor moved down this year without you deciding it should, that's worth an afternoon spent rereading your own dashboard, not someone else's benchmark report.

## Option B — P3 Founder building-in-public — the format we almost didn't test
**Hook** (128 chars)
> Our two best Meta ads are structurally the exact same ad. I didn't notice until I actually sat down and compared them last week.

```
Two ad formats carry most of our winning Meta spend right now: our SafeBuds hero ad (9.70x ROAS this week) and our Comm 2.0 hero ad (11.20x ROAS this week).

Different products, different scripts. But the exact same structure — someone reviewing the product on camera.

I'd been quietly treating that as two proofs of the same idea. It's actually one proof, told twice.

If either format ever fatigues, and every format eventually does, we don't have a real second answer sitting ready. We have the same answer, dressed differently.

So this week we started two formats we've never run in six years. One is an audiologist explaining what open-ear actually does to your hearing, not a customer describing comfort. The other is a plain fear-of-loss hook — what a sealed earbud is doing to you that you can't feel yet.

Neither has real numbers to report. That's not the point of this post.

The point is I nearly didn't run them at all, because the review format is working well enough that testing anything else felt like an unnecessary risk to a good number.

That instinct, protect what's working, don't rock the boat, is exactly how an account ends up with one format and no backup plan.

We funded both tests by reallocating spend off a campaign we'd already killed for buying views nobody bought anything from. Not new budget.

If they fail, we lose nothing we weren't already wasting. If they work, we stop being a one-format account for the first time in six years.

I'll post the real numbers once we have them. Not before.

## Option C — P2 Meta Ads / D2C playbook — the ad we almost killed
**Hook** (118 chars)
> Our worst-performing Meta ad ever, by lifetime ROAS, is this month's best-performing ad. We didn't touch the creative.

```
One of our ES Lite ads has run for over a year. Lifetime, it sits at 4.20x ROAS, comfortably the weakest ad in that product line, the exact kind of number that gets a creative quietly retired.

When ES Lite came back in stock, the obvious move was to leave that ad out of the relaunch and lead with our actual lifetime winners instead.

We put it back in anyway, on a freshly rebuilt audience, mostly because 183 real purchases over its lifetime felt like too much signal to throw away on a number that might have been about the audience, not the ad.

The week we relaunched it: 13.89x ROAS. Cost per purchase down from ₹442 lifetime to ₹142. It briefly became the strongest ad in the entire product line.

Nothing about the video changed. Script, shots, voiceover, all identical.

What changed was the audience it was showing up in front of. The old one was months stale. The new one was rebuilt from the last year of actual buyers.

I keep wanting to read a bad ROAS as a bad ad. Most of the time this month, it's been a bad audience wearing a bad ad's clothes.

Before you retire a creative for good, it's worth asking which number you're actually killing it for, the ad or the audience it's stuck showing up in front of.

## Option D — P4 Product / category — the gap nobody else has closed
**Hook** (142 chars)
> For over a month, we've been the only open-ear brand in India talking about hearing health on Meta. Every competitor is still selling comfort.

```
Every week for over a month, I've checked what boAt and Grodd are actually running as open-ear ads in India.

boAt's message hasn't changed: comfort, all-day wear, "clips on easy." Grodd's hasn't either: price, discount codes, a different comfort claim.

Neither has run a single ad about what open-ear actually protects you from, the hearing damage from hours of sealed earbuds, the fatigue of a driver who can't feel his own ears by hour four.

We have. It's been our whole reason for existing since 2020, when we built India's first open-ear D2C brand around exactly that idea.

For four straight weeks now, saying it hasn't cost us anything on Meta. Our SafeBuds hero ad's cost per purchase has actually improved in the same weeks boAt kept running fresh comfort-led creative.

It would be easy to read that as proof the differentiation is settled and move on.

I don't think it is. Being the only one saying something isn't the same as saying it as well as you could.

So this week we started testing an ad built entirely around an audiologist explaining the mechanism, not a customer describing comfort, pushing the health angle harder instead of coasting on being the only one making it.

The day a competitor runs a real health-education ad in this category is the day I'll know we waited too long to go further.
