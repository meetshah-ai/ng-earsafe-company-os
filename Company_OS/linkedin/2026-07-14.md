```
Our ES Lite earbuds came back in stock. Time to relaunch the ad that made them a hero product the first time.

Before we hit go, I asked one question: is the lookalike audience actually built from recent buyers?

The audience was named "Lookalike -Lite 23 May 26." Sounded fresh. Recently dated.

We traced it back anyway.

The name was a refresh timestamp. The actual customer list underneath it was frozen on 2025-11-18.

We pulled our real purchase data for the last 365 days: 1,247 people bought ES Lite.

966 of them — about 3 out of every 4 — bought after that list was frozen.

We were about to build a lookalike audience, and target it at people who look like our customers, from a list that excluded most of our actual customers.

Not a display bug. Not Meta's fault. A name that sounded current and wasn't.

We're rebuilding the seed list before a single rupee goes out. The relaunch waits.

The lesson isn't about ES Lite. It's that a "refreshed" audience and a "rebuilt" audience are not the same thing, and the label never tells you which one you have.

Check the actual source file. Not the date on the label.
```
