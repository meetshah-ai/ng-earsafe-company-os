## CONFIRMED PATTERNS (promote proven learnings here)
- **Pre-flight canary before spending the report budget** (confirmed 2026-08-13, first proposed 2026-08-10): a single cheap `GET /v2/profiles` call correctly predicts whether a full SP/SB/SD + search-term report sequence is worth attempting. Applied a 4th time this session (2026-08-13, later cycle): canary returned 200 for the first time in 4 cycles, correctly signaling "proceed" — the rule now has evidence on both branches (3× correct STOP, 1× correct GO). Standing rule, proven across 4 consecutive cycles. **Applied a 5th time same-day (this cycle): 200 again, and the credential held stable across two independent report-request batches in one day (4 requests earlier, 5 more this cycle) — auth stability is no longer in question.**
- **3-strikes rule for infra blockers, now itself proven** (2026-08-13): the department's own 2026-08-10 rule — "if a 3rd consecutive cycle hits the identical failure, stop re-diagnosing and treat it as a pure IT ticket" — was invoked for real earlier today (cycle #3) and correctly capped diagnostic spend at 5 calls instead of a full battery. The escalation worked: later the same day, credentials were fixed upstream and the very next attempt (this cycle, #4) succeeded cleanly. Promote this as a standing rule for any future infra blocker of any kind, not just this credential issue — and note the corollary: once an infra blocker clears, don't assume last cycle's data is still representative — re-baseline everything, don't "resume" a stale picture.
- **NEW (2026-08-13): re-baseline fully after any multi-cycle infra outage, don't patch the old baseline.** After 3 blocked cycles (36 days), the account had been completely restructured underneath the department — every campaign named in AZ-001/002/003 (from the 2026-07-08 baseline) no longer exists. Had this cycle tried to "refresh" those specific campaigns instead of pulling everything fresh and diffing the whole campaign list, it would have silently reported on dead campaign IDs. Future cycles recovering from an outage: always diff the full live campaign list against the last-known list before trusting any campaign-specific carry-forward draft.
- **NEW (2026-08-13, later same-day cycle): a single-window matured read is not enough to certify a SCALE draft — always require the *next* matured window before treating a scale call as durable.** `Comm | Generic | 16th July'26 | SP` cleared the scale gate on its first 2-week matured read (14.71×→31.88×, drafted as AZ-020) and was queued as a clean SCALE candidate. The very next matured week (this cycle's Wk1) collapsed to 2.48× on +226% spend — the marginal ROAS on that specific increment was catastrophic, hidden entirely by the campaign's still-healthy trailing-30-day blended number. Root cause was keyword-level: two brand-new zero-return additions (an auto "category" target and a phrase keyword) plus two proven winners inverting/vanishing in the same week. **Standing rule: no SCALE draft should be treated as "confirmed" off a single 2-week comparison — schedule an automatic re-read on the very next matured window before calling it durable, and always be ready to drop one layer to keyword-level the moment a campaign's WoW delta looks off, even if the previous week's number looked great.** This is exactly why the campaign-level-only read this department was doing before (cycle #4 and earlier) was insufficient — it would have kept scaling a campaign that was actively breaking underneath its own aggregate.
- **NEW (2026-08-13, later same-day cycle): blended matured ROAS clearing the 6.0 floor does not mean the account is safe to keep scaling — always compute marginal ROAS on the WoW spend increment itself.** Account-level blended matured ROAS this cycle was 8.54× (comfortably above floor) but the marginal ROAS on the account's own ₹6,672 WoW spend increase was only 2.46× — a number the blended figure completely conceals. Promote "always compute incremental-spend ROAS, not just level ROAS, at both the account and campaign layer" as a standing computation, not an occasional check.
- **NEW (2026-08-26): a 2-consecutive-matured-week SCALE clearance is now proven, 3 times over, not durable enough on its own.** AZ-020/036 (2026-08-13), AZ-032/046 and AZ-018/033/047 (both 2026-08-26) all cleared the standard 2-week gate cleanly and then broke on the very next matured read — two of them collapsing to zero. This is no longer a single cautionary tale; it's the account's dominant SCALE-draft failure mode. Standing rule promoted: treat every 2-week SCALE clearance as provisional by default, and see AZ-058 for the formal 3-week-confirmation test now running against it.
