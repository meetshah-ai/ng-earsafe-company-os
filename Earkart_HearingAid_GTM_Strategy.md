# NG EarSafe × Earkart — Hearing Aid Category Entry
## Market Analysis & Go-to-Market Strategy

*Prepared as a strategy note | June 2026 | Author: Head of Content & Growth, acting as strategy lead*
*Confidential — internal decision document*

---

## 0. THE QUESTION ON THE TABLE

Earkart has offered NG EarSafe its full hearing-care catalogue at **NLC = 50% of MRP** (i.e. a 50% gross margin to us at full MRP). The catalogue spans clinical hearing aids, hearing-protection earplugs, a remote diagnostic device, and accessories.

Two decisions must be separated, because they are not the same business:

1. **Should NG EarSafe sell Earkart's hearing-protection and accessory range?** — A lifestyle/prevention product, on-brand, low-risk.
2. **Should NG EarSafe enter the hearing-aid category?** — A regulated, service-heavy medical business with a different buyer, a different trust bar, and a different cost-to-serve.

This note answers both, with the market evidence to back the call.

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — THE ANSWER FIRST

**The headline:** The 50% margin is real but misleading. A hearing aid is not a 50%-margin *product* — it is a *service business wearing a product's clothes*. The margin pays for audiology, fitting, trials, returns and two years of after-sales. Bolt it onto the EarSafe lifestyle brand naively and we import a cost structure we are not built for and dilute a prevention brand with a "disability/aging" category.

**But there is a genuine, large, and under-served white space** — and NG EarSafe + Earkart is one of the few combinations in India positioned to take it.

Three moves, sequenced:

| Move | What | Risk | Timing | Owner of cost-to-serve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Move 1 — DO NOW** | Sell **Bluup earplugs + accessories** (dehumidifier, wax guards, tips) under EarSafe | Low | This sprint | EarSafe (none clinical) |
| **Move 2 — BUILD** | Launch a **dedicated, online-first "affordable home hearing care" vertical** — *powered by Earkart* — targeting the missing middle (mild-moderate loss, 45-65, bought by adult children). EarSafe owns demand-gen + brand; Earkart owns audiology + fulfilment + warranty. | Medium | 90-day pilot, then scale | **Earkart** (clinical), EarSafe (CAC) |
| **Move 3 — GATE** | Full clinical retail / wide hearing-aid SKU range on the core EarSafe store | High | Only if Move 2 unit economics clear the gate | — reject for now |

**Why this is the right shape:** EarSafe owns the *prevention* end of the hearing continuum (young, urban, "the way you listen is hurting you"). Earkart owns the *correction* end (clinical, treatment, ex-Starkey leadership, India's largest all-brand clinic network, remote diagnostics). These are the two ends of **one journey**. The strategic prize is to be the brand that walks a customer from prevention → screening → correction — without forcing them into a clinic and without the ₹1.5 lakh price tag that defines the incumbents.

## 3. THE INDIA HEARING-HEALTH MARKET

### 3.1 The demand reality (large, growing, structurally untreated)

- **~63 million Indians** have significant hearing loss (≈6.3% of population); hearing disability is the **2nd-most-common disability** in India. Rural prevalence (~32%) > urban (~26%). *(NMJI / IJOO / ResearchGate.)*
- **Penetration is the story:** hearing-aid adoption among those who need it sits in **low single digits — ~1–5%** (vs ~17% WHO global; one industry note puts China + India at ~1%). Only **~5% of older adults** with hearing difficulty report using a device. *(LASI-DAD; Hearing Review.)*
- **The delay:** the average person waits **7–10 years** between onset and first device. Untreated mild loss ≈ 2× dementia risk; moderate ≈ 3×; severe ≈ 5×. The health cost of waiting is now well-evidenced — a powerful, honest content angle.
- **The young flank:** noise-induced hearing loss is rising fast in India's young-adult population (the exact cohort EarSafe already owns). Market reports explicitly flag "India's large young-adult population at risk of NIHL" as **incremental demand**. This is the bridge between our two businesses.

### 3.2 Market size (triangulated — and the dispersion is itself a signal)

Published estimates are *all over the map* — from USD 74M to USD 900M+ for "India hearing aids," and one source quotes a clearly-wrong USD 4.2B (that is global/APAC). **The very fact that credible firms disagree by 10× tells you this is an immature, under-measured, fragmented market** — which is where category-defining brands are built.

Defensible working numbers:
- **India hearing-aid market ≈ ₹1,800–2,400 Cr (~USD 220–290M), growing ~8% CAGR** (Grand View / TechSci / MarketsandMarkets triangulated, discarding outliers).
- Skewed to premium imported devices sold through clinics; the **affordable entry-digital segment (₹8K–30K) is the fastest-growing and least-served** sub-band.

### 3.3 Government & reimbursement tailwind

- **NPPCD** (National Programme for Prevention & Control of Deafness), **Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana** (free aids to BPL seniors), and **Ayushman Bharat** audiology expansion. Earkart already has a **government-business arm** — a separate, large, tender-driven channel we should be aware of but not chase ourselves.

## 4. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

### 4.1 The price architecture (per unit unless noted)

| Tier | Players | Price band | Channel | Who it serves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Premium clinical** | Signia, Phonak, Widex, Oticon, ReSound, Starkey | ₹25,000 – ₹4,00,000+ | Audiologist clinics, fitted | Affluent, severe loss, repeat buyers |
| **Mid clinical** | Same brands, essential lines | ₹50,000 – ₹2,00,000 | Clinics | Moderate loss |
| **Entry digital** | Signia Fast, Philips, **Zenaud (Hearzap/GN)**, regional brands, **Earkart** | ₹10,000 – ₹30,000 | Clinics + online | First-time, value, mild-moderate |
| **Online / commodity** | Amazon/Flipkart no-name amplifiers, JS/Axon-type | ₹500 – ₹5,000 | Marketplace | Desperate / uninformed (mostly poor outcomes) |
| **Global OTC (not yet a formal India category)** | Eargo, Lexie, Sony, Apple AirPods Pro (hearing-aid feature) | $500–1,000/pair | D2C online (US) | Mild-moderate, tech-forward |

### 4.2 The channel structure (the real moat — and the real cost)

- **~90–95% of hearing aids in India are sold through audiologist-led clinics** — independent shops plus chains: **Amplifon** (70+ yrs, merged National Hearing Care), **Hearzap** (360 One-backed, positions as India's #1 chain; launched Zenaud rechargeable ITC with GN, Jan 2025), HearX/others, and thousands of independents.
- **Audiologist scarcity is acute** — a few thousand certified audiologists for 1.4 billion people, concentrated in metros. *This is the structural bottleneck the whole category is throttled by — and exactly what Earkart Omni (remote diagnostics) is built to bypass.*
- **Implication:** Whoever solves "trusted fitting + service without a physical clinic in every town" unlocks the 95% of India the clinics can't reach. That is the category's central unsolved problem.

### 4.3 Regulation

- Hearing aids are **CDSCO Class B medical devices** (MDR 2017). Manufacture/import/sale requires a CDSCO licence. **India has *not* created a formal "OTC / self-fitting" category** like the US FDA did in 2022 — so air-conduction aids are sold online today in a grey-but-tolerated zone. **Earkart, as the licensed manufacturer/importer, carries this compliance** — a major reason to partner rather than import ourselves.

## 5. LIMITATIONS, BARRIERS & THE WHITE SPACE

### 5.1 The four walls suppressing the category

1. **Stigma** — hearing aids = "old / disabled / weak." The single biggest blocker. *(Solvable by: invisible form factors + a youthful, de-medicalised brand voice — EarSafe's core competence.)*
2. **Awareness** — most don't know they have loss, or think aids are only geriatric. *(Solvable by: content + free online screening.)*
3. **Cost** — ₹25K–3L feels absurd to a first-timer. *(Solvable by: Earkart's ₹8–30K range + no-cost EMI.)*
4. **Access** — no clinics, no audiologists in tier-2/3. *(Solvable by: Earkart Omni remote diagnostics + online-first model.)*

**Every one of the four walls maps to a capability EarSafe or Earkart already has.** That is the thesis.

### 5.2 The white space — "the missing middle"

The market is barbelled: premium clinical (₹1L+) at one end, junk Amazon amplifiers (₹500) at the other. **The middle is empty:** the urban/peri-urban Indian with *early-to-moderate* loss who (a) won't enter a clinic (stigma), (b) won't spend ₹1.5L, (c) *will* buy online, and (d) is very often **bought-for by an adult child** (the real Indian buyer — daughter/son researching for a parent).

No one owns: **affordable + invisible + bought online + with a clinical safety net + de-stigmatised brand.** Eargo proved the model in the US; no one has nailed it in India with a *credible clinical backbone*. EarSafe (brand + audience + demand-gen) + Earkart (devices + audiology + remote diagnostics + licence) is a rare fit for it.

|---|---|---|---|

## 7. UNIT ECONOMICS — THE 50% MARGIN, STRESS-TESTED

Take **TINY (₹29,900 MRP, ₹14,950 NLC)** as the hero. The 50% GM looks great until you load the real costs of selling a medical device online:

| Line | Optimistic | Realistic |
|---|---|---|
| Realised selling price (after street discount + EMI subvention) | ₹29,900 | ₹24,900 |
| NLC (cost of device) | (₹14,950) | (₹14,950) |
| **Gross margin** | **₹14,950 (50%)** | **₹9,950 (40%)** |
| Payment / COD / EMI cost (~3–5%) | (₹900) | (₹1,000) |
| Returns & exchange provision (15–25% return rate, amortised) | (₹1,500) | (₹2,800) |
| Audiology + fitting + 4 consults + 2-yr warranty service | *Earkart absorbs* | *Earkart absorbs* |
| Logistics (2-way) | (₹400) | (₹600) |
| **Contribution before CAC** | **₹12,150** | **₹5,550** |
| Allowable CAC to stay profitable | < ₹12,000 | **< ₹5,500** |

**The verdict:** *If Earkart carries audiology + warranty (their offer stack confirms they do), the model can work* — but only if **CAC stays under ~₹5,500 per device** at realistic pricing. That is achievable *only* because EarSafe already owns a warm, hearing-conscious audience and an organic content engine — i.e. our edge is **cheap, qualified demand.** A cold-paid-media-only model would not clear the gate. **This single number — CAC < ₹5,500 — is the make-or-break metric of Move 2 and the gate for Move 3.**

The lower-priced Fame/Radius SKUs have thinner absolute contribution (₹4–10K GM) and should be positioned as EMI-friendly entry / gov-adjacent, not as the paid-acquisition hero.

## 8. THE GO-TO-MARKET STRATEGY

### MOVE 1 — Hearing protection + accessories, under EarSafe, NOW

**What:** List **Bluup earplugs (₹1,900 / ₹2,900)**, LED Dehumidifier, wax guards, tips on the EarSafe store and content calendar.
**Why:** Zero clinical risk, no fitting, no CDSCO drama (protection devices), and **perfectly on-brand** — "safe listening" is literally a brand hashtag. Bluup is the *prevention* product for the existing 25–45 audience (sleep, focus, travel, concerts, work — the exact occasions our Tribe D / B already live in). 50% GM, impulse price point, recurring (tips/wax/batteries) attach.
**Positioning:** *Not* "earplugs" → "**the other half of listening well.** Sometimes the most you can hear is silence." Mode 1/Mode 4 captions. Pairs with the open-ear story: *we protect your ears whether sound is going in or being kept out.*
**Risk:** Low. **Decision: proceed this sprint.**

### MOVE 2 — "Affordable home hearing care, powered by Earkart" — the real prize

**The model — a demand-gen marketplace, not a clinic:**

1. **EarSafe = top of funnel.** Use our audience, content engine, SEO and paid muscle to drive the **"Do you (or your parent) hear as well as you think?"** conversation. Lead magnet = **free online hearing screening** (Earkart Omni / remote audiology). This is honest, on-mission, and shareable.
2. **Earkart = the backend.** Qualified leads flow to Earkart's senior audiologists for remote diagnosis (Omni), device recommendation, fitting, the ₹25K benefit stack (free insurance, 0% EMI, 4 consults, exchange), and 2-yr warranty service.
3. **Revenue split:** EarSafe earns the device margin (50% NLC structure) on units it sources/sells; Earkart carries the clinical cost-to-serve and recurring service. Exact split to be negotiated — *EarSafe must NOT take device-margin without Earkart contractually owning fitting + warranty + returns liability.* (This is the deal's central protection.)

**Sub-brand & naming:** House it as **"EarSafe Care"** (or co-branded *"EarSafe Care, powered by Earkart"*) — adjacent to, but visually and tonally distinct from, the lifestyle feed. Separate landing pages, separate Meta pixel/campaigns, separate IG highlight or even a sister handle. **Never** run a hearing-aid hard-sell in the main EarSafe Reels feed.

**Target segment (priority order):**
1. **The adult child** (30–45, urban, English/Hindi, already partly in our audience) buying for a parent (55–75) — *primary CAC target; they search, they pay, they have low stigma.*
2. **The early-onset self-buyer** (45–60, mild loss, professional, won't enter a clinic) — TINY/Radius, invisible + rechargeable hooks.
3. **NIHL-aware younger flank** (our existing 25–45) — mostly screening/prevention now, a future correction pipeline.

**Positioning line:** *"Hearing care that comes to you. Tested at home, fitted by a real audiologist, at a price that makes sense."* De-medicalised, dignity-first, anti-stigma. Lean on TINY's **invisible + rechargeable** story to kill the stigma objection.

**Channel mix:**
- **SEO/AEO** — own "hearing aid price India," "rechargeable invisible hearing aid," "hearing test online," "hearing aid for parents." (High-intent, the clinics rank poorly with thin content — a known gap.)
- **Performance (Meta + Google)** — interest + intent targeting to the adult-child buyer; lookalikes off our existing list. Track to **CAC < ₹5,500.**
- **Content** — long-form trust content (the 7-year delay, dementia link, "is it loss or just wax?"), founder/audiologist authority (Earkart's ex-Starkey pedigree is gold here), real-customer stories.
- **WhatsApp** — our best-converting channel per existing data; ideal for the high-touch consult booking + EMI nudge.

**Pilot design (90 days):** One state/metro cluster, hero = TINY + Radius 16, free online screening funnel, hard cap on spend, single success metric: **CAC < ₹5,500 at ≥ 40% realised GM and < 25% returns.** Read at Day 45 and Day 90.

### MOVE 3 — Full clinical range / wide SKU retail — GATED, reject for now

Only pursue broad SKU range, severe-loss devices, or any physical-clinic ambition **if Move 2 clears its unit-economics gate.** Until then it is capital-intensive, off-brand, and competes head-on with Amplifon/Hearzap's clinic moat — a fight we should not pick.
